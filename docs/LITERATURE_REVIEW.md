# Predicting Antibody Developability from Sequence: Background and Literature Review

## 1. Why developability matters, and why it is predicted from sequence

A therapeutic antibody can bind its target with picomolar affinity and still fail: aggregating at formulation concentration, self-associating into a viscous solution that cannot be delivered subcutaneously, binding off-target surfaces and clearing fast, or degrading chemically on the shelf. These are *developability* failures, and they are expensive precisely because they surface late — after a lead has been selected, humanised, and committed to.

The canonical empirical picture comes from Jain et al. [jain2017pnas], who expressed 137 clinical-stage antibodies as isotype-matched IgG1 and ran twelve biophysical assays on all of them. The headline result is a gradient rather than a cliff: 65% (31/48) of approved antibodies carried zero warning flags, against 40% (19/47) of phase-2 molecules, and roughly 32% (15/47) of phase-2 molecules carried more than two flags. No single assay separates approved from unapproved cleanly — for hydrophobic interaction chromatography (HIC) retention time specifically, the approved-versus-unapproved comparison gives p = 1.0 [jain2017pnas, Table S2]. The practical reading is that developability assays are **risk filters for triage**, not approval predictors, and that is the frame this project adopts.

The case for predicting these properties from sequence rather than measuring them is one of cost and position in the workflow. Assays need purified material at milligram scale; a discovery campaign produces thousands of candidates before any of them is expressed. A sequence-only model can rank *in silico*, before the expression step. The argument is also one of reproducibility: Park & Izadi [park2024] show that structure-derived surface descriptors are not stable across structure-prediction methods — CDR_APBS_pos agrees at R² = 0.85 between MOE and ABodyBuilder2 but only R² = 0.34 between ABodyBuilder2 and DeepAb, and CDR_HPATCH_BM at R² = 0.60–0.78 depending on the pair. Flag assignments change with the modelling software. A sequence-only pipeline has no such degree of freedom.

## 2. The measurement landscape, and why mixing assays is invalid

The twelve assays in [jain2017pnas] measure different physics and are reported in incompatible units: PSR (unitless, 0–1), AC-SINS (Δλmax, nm), CSI-BLI (nm), CIC (min), HIC (min), SMAC (min), SGAC-SINS (mM (NH₄)₂SO₄), BVP and poly-specificity ELISA (fold-over-background), accelerated stability (% monomer loss/day), Fab Tm (°C), and HEK titer (mg/L). Only ten carry warning thresholds; Fab Tm and HEK titer were explicitly excluded because "the values were close to normally distributed and within an acceptable range for development" [jain2017pnas].

The flagging convention matters and is routinely misread. Verbatim: "In keeping with the threshold used by Lipinski et al., we chose the cutoff of 10% worst values among approved drugs as a threshold for a warning flag." The percentile is computed over the **48 approved antibodies only**, not over all 137. The HIC threshold is **> 11.7 ± 0.6 min** (bootstrap error, R `boot`), alongside PSR > 0.27 ± 0.06, AC-SINS > 11.8 ± 6.2 nm, CSI > 0.01 ± 0.02, CIC > 10.1 ± 0.5 min, SMAC > 12.8 ± 1.2 min, SGAC-SINS < 370 ± 133 mM, BVP > 4.3 ± 2.2, poly-ELISA > 1.9 ± 1.0, and AS slope > 0.08 ± 0.03 %/day [jain2017pnas, Table 1].

Even within one assay, retention time is **platform-specific, not a physical constant**. HIC retention depends on ligand chemistry, salt identity and concentration, gradient length, and flow rate. Jain's platform uses a Sepax Proteomix Butyl-NP5 column, mobile phase A = 1.8 M (NH₄)₂SO₄ + 0.1 M sodium phosphate pH 6.5, a linear 20-minute gradient at 1 mL/min. Jetha et al. [jetha2018] use a Tosoh TSKgel Ether-5PW, 1.5 M (NH₄)₂SO₄, and a 30-minute gradient — and their retention times span 21.3–30.0 min against Jain's ~8.5–16. Those numbers are **not on the same scale** and must not be pooled as a single regression target. Pooling requires either rank-only training or an explicit per-study offset/scale term.

A separate quantification: the assays themselves are reproducible. Arsiwala et al. (PROPHET-Ab) re-measured 246 antibodies on an independent platform and recovered Spearman ρ = 0.97 against Jain for HIC (n = 133) and ρ = 0.88–0.89 for AC-SINS [arsiwala2025]. **Inferred, not published:** this implies a label-noise ceiling near ρ ≈ 0.97 for HIC and ≈ 0.88 for AC-SINS — assay noise is not what limits HIC prediction.

## 3. Datasets used here, with provenance

**Primary — Jain et al. 2017 (n = 137).** 48 FDA-approved, 42 phase-3 or phase-2/3, 47 phase-2; 124 κ / 13 λ; 58 fully human, 67 humanized, 12 non-human V-regions [jain2017pnas]. The paper does not print the HIC min/max in text (it lives in Dataset S3). **Computed by us, not published:** from the FLAb redistribution at `data/raw/FLAb/data/aggregation/jain2017biophyscial_HICRT.csv` (n = 137), the range is 8.5–25.0 min, mean 10.33, median 9.9; applying the 11.7-min threshold flags **15/137 (10.9%)**. The paper publishes no per-assay flag tally, so that count is ours. We note plainly that the Jain 2017 SI Appendix PDF could not be retrieved during this review (pnas.org returns 403; the Europe PMC fullTextXML endpoint returns 500), so these distributional figures are derived from the redistributed Dataset S3 rather than quoted from the source.

**The primary endpoint is itself right-censored.** Exactly 2 of 137 HIC values sit at 25.0 min. An independent ML study on the same data states these "were arbitrarily assigned a value of 25 due to exceeding the maximum measurement time" and excluded them from training [hebditch2019]. *Attribution caveat:* one of our three literature sweeps attributed this record (PMC6967001 / bioRxiv 625830 / PeerJ 7:e8199, title "Charge and hydrophobicity are key features in sequence-trained machine learning models…") to Rabia, Tessier and colleagues; the other two attribute the same record to Hebditch & Warwicker. The bioRxiv number, PeerJ number and title all match Hebditch & Warwicker, so we cite it as [hebditch2019] and flag the conflict rather than resolve it silently.

**External cohort — Jain et al. 2024 (n = 43).** FLAb labels this "Germline Fv antibodies," which is misleading. These are not germline-reverted clinical mAbs: they are a diverse subset of 334 IgG1s from an Adimab hen-egg-lysozyme discovery campaign, stratified by VH germline family and deliberately biased toward the tails of the PSR/AC-SINS/pI distributions, with framework and CDR1/CDR2 largely unmutated from germline; all 43 carry in vivo PK in hFcRn Tg32 mice [jain2024]. Decisively for us, the HIC method is **verbatim identical** to 2017 — same column, same 1.8 M ammonium sulfate system, same 20-minute gradient, same flow rate, same lab, with the methods stating assays were "performed as previously described." The empirical distributions agree (**computed by us:** n = 43, 8.4–16.0 min, mean 10.69, median 9.9 — identical median to 2017). We therefore report **MAE and RMSE in minutes** on this set, not Spearman alone.

**Domain-shift stress test — Jetha et al. 2018 (n = 97).** One parent, NB0268 (anti-integrin α11, phage display), with 39 single mutants, 24 Y30×Y54 doubles and 33 Y54×Y102 doubles [jetha2018]. Note the year is **2018** (mAbs 10(6):890–900), despite the FLAb filename reading `jetha2019`. Censoring is explicit in the paper: non-eluting antibodies were "assigned an elution time of 30 minutes, representing the maximum value possible," and the parent itself "did not elute from the column during the 30 min method." **Computed by us:** exactly 30/97 values (30.9%) sit at 30.00 min. A plain regression on this set is inappropriate; a censored (Tobit) loss or explicit exclusion is required.

**Zero-shot baseline — FLAb.** 17 landscapes, 13,384 fitness values, 8 studies [flab2024]. Its aggregation block *is* the Jain 2017 panel (6 assays × 137 = 822 values), labelled "Wittrup …" in the supplementary tables after the senior author — a reader should not mistake these for a separate cohort. One discrepancy worth recording: the preprint lists six Jain assays in the aggregation block, but our local FLAb checkout contains only five (SMAC absent), suggesting a commit mismatch.

## 4. Prior predictive approaches

**Biophysical profilers.** TAP [raybould2019] computes five Fv metrics from a homology model — total CDR length, PSH (patches of surface hydrophobicity, Kyte–Doolittle), PPC, PNC, and SFvCSP — with amber flags at the extreme 5% of the clinical-stage distribution (242 therapeutics: the Jain 137 plus 105 further phase-II+ molecules; thresholds re-derived on 851 Fvs as of Feb 2025) and red flags for values never observed in a therapeutic. **TAP reports no correlation coefficient against HIC, AC-SINS, or any assay.** It is an outlier flag, and it cannot honestly be cited as a quantitative regression baseline. Worse for its hydrophobicity claim: the paper argues scale choice is immaterial because scales agree at R² ≥ 0.91 in the CDR vicinity, while Park & Izadi [park2024] report the opposite — "the widely used KD scale shows weak correlations with all other scales" — and find CDR_PSH the *worst*-performing hydrophobicity descriptor against Jain HIC, beaten by CDR_HPATCH_BM (Pearson R² ≈ 0.55–0.60 on a single snapshot). Waibl et al. [waibl2022] independently find Kyte–Doolittle and Eisenberg poor for HIC relative to HIC-optimised (Jain, Meek) and Wimley–White scales. These numeric comparisons appear only as bar plots in the respective figures and were not tabulated; we do not quote point values for them.

**Surrogate models for MD descriptors.** DeepSCM [lai2022deepscm] predicts the spatial charge map from sequence at r = 0.90 (N = 1320) and classified 37/38 therapeutic mAbs correctly for viscosity; DeepSP [kalejaye2024deepsp] predicts 30 MD-derived spatial properties with test r = 0.76–0.96 (mean 0.87, N = 2053). Both predict *computed* labels, not assay endpoints — a much easier task, and a common source of apples-to-oranges comparison. Neither reports HIC or AC-SINS.

**Supervised ML on Jain-type panels.** Jain, Boland et al. [jain2017bioinf] framed HIC as *classification*: a random forest predicts per-residue CDR SASA from sequence (Pearson 0.85–0.97 per CDR, MAE 4.6%), then LASSO logistic regression classifies "delayed" retention (> 2 min later than reference; ~26% of 5,089 in-house antibodies), reaching cross-validated ROC-AUC 0.85 (Brier 0.135), validated on 112 clinical mAbs. Hebditch & Warwicker [hebditch2019] is the cleanest sequence-only regression comparator: 35 Fv sequence features, all 12 Jain assays, 50× repeated 10-fold CV, with HIC R² = 0.391 (MAE 0.702) — their best assay — and AC-SINS R² = 0.268. Karlberg et al. [karlberg2020] is rare in using a genuine external split (80/20 Kennard–Stone on 81 Jain IgG1-κ mAbs): sequence-only R² = 0.25, homology-model R² = −0.08, MD-relaxed R² = 0.63 (RMSE ±1.32 min). PROPERMAB [li2025propermab] is the current best published Jain-panel HIC number: ElasticNet over 9 sequence + 26 structure features, nested leave-one-out CV on 135 mAbs, Pearson r = 0.71 / Spearman ρ = 0.75, with hydrophobic patch area near the CDRs the strongest single feature. The same paper is a useful cautionary note: it failed to reproduce an earlier claim that SCM predicts IgG4 viscosity (ρ = 0.12 against ~0.80 previously reported).

**Protein language models.** FLAb [flab2024] evaluates AntiBERTy, IgLM, ProtGPT2, ProGen2 (seven sizes), ProteinMPNN, ESM-IF and Rosetta energy zero-shot, scoring by mean per-residue (pseudo-)perplexity. On Jain HIC, Pearson r ranges from −0.08 to 0.24 — AntiBERTy 0.05, IgLM 0.01, ProGen2-OAS −0.08, ProteinMPNN 0.09, ESM-IF 0.24, Rosetta 0.05 — and Spearman similarly (ProteinMPNN and ESM-IF both 0.24, everything else |ρ| ≤ 0.18). The authors conclude that "the average absolute PCC is below 0.2" on aggregation. With n = 137 the two-sided p = 0.05 boundary is |r| ≈ 0.168, so ESM-IF is plausibly the only significant entry; FLAb bolds significant cells but the bolding was lost in text extraction, so we do not claim which were significant. The successor benchmark [flab2025] reports a zero-shot best of ρ = 0.29 (BioPython net charge at pH 7.4) with an all-model average of −0.07, and a few-shot best of ρ = 0.66; its figures should be verified against the published version before quoting. Supervised PLM work on proprietary data is far stronger: Amini et al. [amini2026] on 18,540 Biogen antibodies report random-split Spearman of 0.75–0.85 for HIC (n = 1,792), 0.55–0.75 for PSR, 0.40–0.50 for AC-SINS — but MMseqs2 cluster-based splits drop all three by ~0.2–0.3. Pretrained general ESM2 beat the antibody-specific IgBert/IgT5 for HIC; model size from 8M to 650M did not help; linear regressors beat ensembles under shift.

## 5. What drives HIC retention mechanistically

HIC runs in high kosmotropic salt with a descending gradient: salt strips ordered water from apolar surface, the protein binds a weakly hydrophobic butyl or phenyl ligand, and elutes as salt falls. Retention therefore tracks **solvent-exposed** apolar surface — buried Leu/Ile contribute nothing. Because IgG framework surfaces are highly conserved, variance is dominated by the CDR loops and their anchors; [jain2017bioinf] recovered this directly by predicting exposure first and only then weighting amino-acid propensities, finding aromatics (Trp, Tyr, Phe) most positive, aliphatics (Leu, Ile, Val, Met) next, and Arg/Lys/Asp/Glu negative, with framework features and germline family adding nothing. Their learned scale correlates at |r| > 0.9 with Black–Mould and organic-solvent transfer free-energy scales [blackmould1991]. Hebditch & Warwicker [hebditch2019] recover the same signature from Fv composition alone — aromatic content positive, absolute charge negative, non-linearly, with charge mattering mainly at long retention times where screening is weakest.

The cleanest residue-level case is MEDI-1912: exposed W30 and F31 (CDR-H1) plus L56 (CDR-H2) drove self-association, and the W30S/F31T/L56T triple mutant raised concentrability from 1.4 to 29 mg/mL with retained affinity [dobson2016]. Waibl et al. [waibl2021] add the reason static descriptors have a ceiling: effective hydrophobicity is a property of the CDR-loop *ensemble*, not one conformer — though, awkwardly, Park & Izadi [park2024] find that MD conformational sampling "consistently reduces the performance of all structure models in predicting the HIC assay."

## 6. Known chemical liability motifs

The standard panel (Jarasch lineage [jarasch2015], consolidated as LAP [satlawa2024]) is sequence-computable: N-glycosylation sequon `N[^P][ST]` (and rarer `N[^P]C`); deamidation high `N[GS]`, moderate `N[AHNT]`, low `[STK]N`; isomerization `D[DGHST]`; fragmentation `DP` (high), `TS`, `NP`; oxidation at any `M` or `W`; unpaired cysteine (odd Cys count outside the conserved IMGT 23/104 pairs); glycation `K[DE]`/`[DE]K`; and a low-risk cross-reactivity set `RGD|RYD|KGD|NGR|LDV|DGE|GPR`.

Two qualifications are essential. First, **none of these are hydrophobicity liabilities** — they are chemical-stability and heterogeneity risks, and only free Cys and glycan sequons bear indirectly on aggregation. They belong in a report as an orthogonal flag channel, not as HIC features. Second, raw motif counts are poor features: Lu et al. [lu2019] measured actual modification in 131 clinical mAbs under forced degradation (pH 8.5/40 °C/1 wk for deamidation; pH 5.5/40 °C/2 wk for isomerization; 5% threshold) and found 67% of NG but only 21% of NS sites deamidated, 36% of DG and 13% of DS isomerised, with deamidation enriched in CDR-H2/L1 and isomerization in CDR-H3/H2/L1. The n+1 effect is ~20× (GGNGG t½ = 1.03 d vs GGNAG 21.1 d) [irudayanathan2022]. LAP [satlawa2024] concludes that roughly **60% of naively flagged motifs are benign** once germline, therapeutic and structural context is applied.

## 7. Honest benchmark positioning

| Zero-shot, developability generally | best ρ = 0.29; all-model mean ρ = −0.07 | [flab2025] |
| Sequence features, Jain 137, 50×10-fold CV | HIC R² = 0.391; AC-SINS R² = 0.268 | [hebditch2019] |
| Sequence-only, Jain 81, external Kennard–Stone split | R² = 0.25 (MD-relaxed structures 0.63) | [karlberg2020] |
| Sequence + structure features, Jain 135, nested LOOCV | HIC r = 0.71, ρ = 0.75 | [li2025propermab] |
| PLM embeddings, 1,792 internal HIC, random split | ρ = 0.75–0.85 (−0.2 to −0.3 under cluster split) | [amini2026] |
| Blinded single-platform competition, 80 test | ρ = 0.708 hydrophobicity; 0.31–0.39 all others | [ginkgo2026] |
| Cross-lab assay reproducibility (noise ceiling) | HIC ρ = 0.97; AC-SINS ρ = 0.88–0.89 | [arsiwala2025] |

**Inferred, not published** (our triangulation across the rows above): for a sequence-only model on ~100–150 *diverse* clinical antibodies under an honest held-out or leave-one-out protocol, Spearman **0.45–0.65 is solid**, **0.65–0.75 matches or beats the best published structure-feature model**, and anything **above ~0.80 should trigger a leakage audit** unless the panel is low-diversity. For AC-SINS the same reasoning gives 0.30–0.50 as good and > 0.55 as exceeding everything published on a diverse panel. Also inferred: squaring Hebditch's R² gives r ≈ 0.63 (HIC) and ≈ 0.52 (AC-SINS) only if R² is the squared correlation of predicted against observed; the paper reports neither r nor ρ. A 2026 bioRxiv preprint reporting R² = 0.916 for fine-tuned IgBert on 359 internal HIC sequences is **not** comparable — the split protocol and sequence diversity are unverified, and such a panel almost certainly contains near-duplicate variants.

Finally, the strongest published evidence on transfer: a HIC model reporting R² = 0.6 on 152 mAbs fell to R² = 0.21 on 64 clinical mAbs, and descriptors recomputed with different modelling software agreed only at R² = 0.31–0.69 [jain2023risks].

## 8. Gaps this project addresses

(i) **No published sequence-only PLM-embedding regressor reports Spearman for HIC on the Jain 137 panel.** Our three sweeps found none. That is a genuine gap — and it also means no like-for-like comparator exists, so we benchmark against [li2025propermab] (structure features, ρ = 0.75) and [hebditch2019] (sequence features, R² = 0.391) directly. (ii) **Censoring is handled explicitly** rather than ignored: the two 25.0-min Jain points and the 30/97 Jetha ceiling values. (iii) **Absolute-error reporting on a true external cohort** is defensible because the 2024 HIC method is identical to 2017. (iv) **A domain-shift test** on a different column chemistry, which most published models never attempt.

## 9. Limitations acknowledged up front

The Jain 2017 SI PDF was not retrievable, so our distributional statistics come from the FLAb redistribution of Dataset S3 and are labelled inferred throughout. Sharma's often-quoted charge thresholds (Fv net charge pH 5.5 between 0 and +6.2; FvCSP and hydrophobicity-index cutoffs) could not be read from the PNAS source and are not quoted here as hard numbers [sharma2014]. The reported Jetha in-study correlation disagrees between our sweeps — 0.69 (and 0.62 transferred to the Jain 137) versus 0.46 — and we do not resolve it; the transferred-model figure of r = 0.35 (p = 0.00044, n = 97) in [hebditch2019] is the one we treat as reliable. A Thorsteinson-lineage result (charge descriptors at ρ = −0.11 AC-SINS, −0.04 HIC) reached us only via secondary literature and was not verified against the primary table. Amini's Spearman values are read from empirical-CDF figures, not tables. Beyond sourcing, three substantive ceilings apply: n = 137 is small and diverse, so CV variance is large and CV has been shown to systematically exceed held-out performance in a blinded setting [ginkgo2026]; single descriptors separate clinical from repertoire antibodies poorly, so interpretability claims from feature weights must stay modest [negron2022]; and sequence-only features cannot resolve solvent exposure or loop flexibility, which is exactly what HIC retention responds to [waibl2021].
